Contacts get duplicated easily: a person writes from two numbers,
sends you an email, or reached you twice through different channels. Brote detects
these duplicates and lets you join them.

## How it detects duplicates

Brote groups contacts by:

- **Exact phone** (best signal, almost always means the same person)
- **Exact email** (case-insensitive)
- **Widget `visitor_id`** (same browser returning across sessions)
- **Normalized name** (lowercase, no accents, no extra spaces) —
  but only when the contact has a `phone` or `email`. Without that data
  (anonymous widget visitors), Brote does NOT use the name to detect
  duplicates.

If the same group shows up under two different criteria (e.g. phone + name),
only the most reliable match is shown: phone > email > visitor_id > name.

### Anonymous web visitors

When someone writes through the embedded widget (without leaving a name or
contact info), Brote saves them as `"Visitante web"` with a `visitor_id`
UUID generated in their browser. These contacts **are not duplicates of each other
even if they share the name** — they're different people.

Brote recognizes them because they're on the `ANONYMOUS_NAMES` blacklist
(`Visitante web`, `Web visitor`, `Anonymous`, `Sin nombre`, etc) and
excludes them from name matching.

If you want to turn an anonymous visitor into an identified contact,
edit them from the Inbox side panel and add a phone or email.
When the customer comes back and leaves their info, Brote won't confuse them with
other anonymous visitors.

## Resolving duplicates

1. Go to **Settings → Contacts** → "Detected duplicates" section.
2. For each group, it shows the contacts with their lifecycle stage,
   contact info and creation date.
3. Click **"Keep this one"** on the winner → Brote merges the rest
   into it.

## What happens when you merge

The winner (`to_id`) keeps all its fields intact. The losers
(`from_id`):

- **Conversations**, **tickets**, **notes**, **tasks**, **tags**,
  **custom fields** and **payments** are reassigned to the winner.
- **Lifecycle history** is preserved — every stage change
  from each contact moves to the winner.
- **Empty fields** on the winner are filled with the loser's data
  (e.g. the winner had no email, the loser did → the winner ends up
  with that email).
- **Tags and notes** are merged (the union of both sets).
- **Metadata** of the winner takes precedence — the loser is stored in
  `metadata.merged_from` for auditing.
- Finally, the loser is deleted.

<Aside type="caution">
The operation is **irreversible**. Before merging many, validate with
1-2 that the result is what you expect. If you made a mistake, message us
and we'll look at the logs to reconstruct.
</Aside>

## Common cases

**Same customer with two phones**: they switched from Movistar to Entel and
now there are 2 contacts. Brote doesn't detect them automatically because the
numbers are different — you'll have to do the merge manually from the
detail view.

**Customer wrote via WhatsApp and via Instagram**: you end up with two contacts
on different channels. If they share an exact email or name, Brote detects them;
otherwise you also have to merge manually.

**Customer with the full name in uppercase and another in lowercase**: Brote
normalizes names, so it groups these on its own.

## Reporting

Each merge's history lives in the winning contact's `metadata.merged_from[]`
(an array with the lost UUIDs) + `metadata.merged_at` (timestamp).
Useful if you audit how many duplicates you resolved this month.
